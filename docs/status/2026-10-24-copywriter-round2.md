# Copywriter — round 2 (2026-10-24)

Agent: copywriter (Cass). Status: done. Changes committed to `week1/privacy-page` (commit 5599865).

## Changes made

### index.html — headline

Old: "Why did your client's search traffic drop?"
New: "Turn a Search Console export into a client-ready traffic report"

Reason: the original is a question, which works as a hook but doesn't convey what Faevra does. The new headline is a direct, active description of the product action — upload data, get a report — that also contains the keywords "Search Console export" and "traffic report". It follows the pattern the audience already uses when they describe the problem to clients.

### index.html — lead paragraph

Old: "Faevra turns a Google Search Console export into a plain-English traffic-drop report you can send straight to your client."
New: "Upload a Google Search Console export and get back a plain-English breakdown of why traffic dropped — written so you can send it straight to your client."

Reason: the new version uses second-person active voice ("you upload / you get back") and gives a sharper sense of the exchange: what goes in, what comes out. "Breakdown of why traffic dropped" is more concrete than "traffic-drop report".

### index.html — "What the report tells you" → "What you get"

Section heading shortened. Benefit bullets given bold lead-ins:
- "The drop, pinpointed." — sets up the diagnostic framing
- "What changed." — positions, CTR, timing
- "Clear next steps." — written for clients, not just the SEO practitioner

Reason: the original bullets were three passive sentences. The bold lead-ins give scannable entry points; the copy rules disallow invented numbers so none were added.

### index.html — CTA note

Old: "Free for one client site while we are starting out."
New: "Free for one client site while we are in early access."

Reason: "starting out" can read as uncertain. "Early access" is a recognised phrase that implies structured testing, which suits the positioning better.

### Step 3 of "How it works"

Old: "You tell us what was useful and what was not."
New: "You tell us what worked and what to improve."

Reason: "what was not" is slightly abrupt; "what to improve" is forward-looking and matches the feedback loop the pilot is designed to create.

## Not changed

- Legal page copy — not in scope for copywriter; legal language set by the legal draft process.
- "Who it is for" — accurate and plain; no invented detail to add.
- CTA button text — "Request a free report" is clear and direct.

## Rules applied

- UK English throughout.
- No invented numbers, testimonials or customer counts.
- No turnaround-time promises (none were in the original; none added).
- Contact email not added (mailbox not yet set up).
